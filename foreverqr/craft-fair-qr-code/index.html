A QR code sign for your craft fair booth

Plenty of people who stop at a craft fair or holiday market table don’t buy on the spot. They pick something up, say they’ll come back, and walk on. A QR code on your booth sign gives them a way to find you again later: your shop, your Instagram, the next market you’ll be at. It goes on a sign you’ll set up at every show this season, and on the tags of everything you sell, so it has to keep working. If it runs through a QR company’s redirect and the trial ends, every sign and tag you printed stops working at once.

Make a booth QR sign

Use a static code

A static code stores your web address in the pattern itself. A phone camera reads it and goes straight to your page, with no QR company in between, so nothing can expire. Many “free” generators make dynamic codes instead: the pattern holds their short link, and scans only reach you while your account is active. Every code ForeverQR makes is static, and there’s no account to sign up for.

Already have codes on last year’s tags? Scan one and look at the address your phone shows before it opens. If it’s the generator’s domain rather than your shop’s, it’s dynamic. Why did my QR code stop working? explains what to do.

What to link to

You can’t change where a static code points after printing, so point it at a page you control and keep that page current. Good choices:

  • Your online shop. Use the shop’s main address, not a single product that might sell out.
  • A simple links page on your own website with the shop, your social accounts, your upcoming market dates and a custom-order form. This is the most flexible option: when your schedule changes, you edit the page and the sign stays the same.
  • Your Instagram profile, if that’s where you post new work. The Instagram QR code guide covers the exact link to use.
  • A mailing-list sign-up (“Get an email before the spring market”). A free form works; the event check-in guide shows a simple setup.

One code on the sign is easier to scan than three codes side by side, and customers don’t have to guess which one to use. Put the code on the price tags, hang tags or packaging too, so a gift that’s unwrapped in January still leads back to you. The product packaging guide covers small labels.

Payments: use your payment app’s own code

If you take payment by phone, use the QR code your payment provider gives you rather than making one yourself, because theirs is tied to your account. Venmo, for example, gives business profiles a QR code that customers scan to pay you, and you can download and print it as a PDF. Square lets you share a Payment Link as a QR code.

One caution if you print a code for a payment link: the code is only as permanent as the link. Square says that once a payment link is deleted or deactivated it becomes unavailable to anyone you shared it with. Before you print it on a sign you’ll reuse, check that the link is one you’ll keep active, and test it at the start of each market.

Keep the payment code and the “find us later” code visibly separate, with clear labels like “Pay here” and “Shop online”, so nobody scans the wrong one at the till.

Size and placement

WhereSuggested width
Hang tag, price tag or business cardAt least 2 cm (0.8 in)
Sign on the table, scanned from about 1 m (3 ft)About 10 cm (4 in)
Banner or sign read from the aisle, about 2 m (6 ft)About 20 cm (8 in)

These come from the common rule of thumb that a code scans from about ten times its own width. The generator’s printable sign is 6 × 8 in with a code about 4.3 in (11 cm) wide, which suits a tabletop stand. It also prints your caption and the web address underneath, so anyone whose camera won’t cooperate can type it.

  • Put it at eye or hand height. A sign flat on the table at the back of the display is hard to scan over your stock. A small easel or acrylic stand at the front corner works better.
  • Watch for glare. Glossy lamination and plastic sleeves reflect overhead lights and the sun. Matte paper or a matte laminate scans more reliably. Test the sign where it will actually stand.
  • Outdoors, plan for weather. Laminate or sleeve the sign, weigh down or clip the stand, and keep a spare printed copy in your kit. Wet, curled or creased paper often won’t scan.
  • Keep the white border. Leave a clear margin about four squares wide on every side. Don’t let the code run into a patterned background or your logo artwork.
  • Dark on light. Stick to a dark code on a white or pale background. Seasonal red-on-green looks festive and scans poorly.
  • Say what it does. “Shop online and see where we’ll be next” gets more scans than a bare code.

Step by step

  1. Decide where the code goes: your shop or a links page you can edit.
  2. Open the generator with the button above and paste the full address.
  3. Scan the on-screen preview with your phone to check it opens the right page.
  4. Set the caption, then download the printable sign, plus an SVG or high-resolution PNG for tags and cards.
  5. Print one sign and one tag, and test both with an iPhone and an Android phone from the distance customers will stand.
  6. At each market, give the sign a quick test scan when you set up. Between shows, update the page, not the code.

FAQ

Will a free QR code on my booth sign stop working?

A static code won’t, because your web address is stored in the pattern itself. Many free generators make dynamic codes that go through their own short link, and those can stop working when a trial or plan ends, which breaks every sign and tag you printed.

Can I use a ForeverQR code to take payments at my booth?

Use the QR code your payment app gives you for payments, because it is tied to your account. Venmo business profiles and Square Payment Links both offer one. Use a ForeverQR code for everything else: your shop, Instagram and mailing list.

How big should the QR code on a craft fair sign be?

A code scans from about ten times its own width, so a code 10 cm (4 in) wide works from about 1 m away, across a typical table. A hang tag or business card needs at least 2 cm (0.8 in).

Do I need a new QR code for each market?

No. Point one code at a page you control, such as your shop or a links page, and change that page when your stock, dates or offers change. The same printed sign works all season.
