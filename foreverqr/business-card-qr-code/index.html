Guide · Business cards

Put a contact QR code on your business card

A contact QR code lets someone save your details without typing them. Done well, it’s small, scans first time and never depends on a website staying online.

Opens the generator with Contact selected. Fill in only the fields you want on the card, then download an SVG for your printer.

Make a contact QR code

Two ways to do it

1. A vCard code (recommended for most people)

The code contains a small contact card in the standard vCard 3.0 format (RFC 2426), the same format phones use to share contacts:

BEGIN:VCARD
VERSION:3.0
N:Rivera;Sam;;;
FN:Sam Rivera
ORG:Rivera Plumbing
TEL;TYPE=CELL:+15551234567
EMAIL:sam@riveraplumbing.com
END:VCARD

Current iPhone and Android camera apps recognise this and offer to add the contact. It works offline, involves no third party, and can never expire.

2. A link to a profile page

The code holds a web address, like your LinkedIn profile or a page on your company site. This keeps the code small and lets you change details later, but it only works while that page exists, and the person still has to tap “add contact” themselves. Use a website link code for this.

Step by step

  1. Open the generator with the button above. Contact is already selected.
  2. Fill in your name, company, job title, mobile and email. Add your website if you like.
  3. Leave the address blank unless clients visit you; it adds a lot of data.
  4. Scan the preview with your own phone and check the contact it offers to save: name order, phone format (use + and your country code) and email.
  5. Download the SVG. It’s a vector file, so your printer can scale it without blurring.

Keep the code small

Every character you add makes the pattern denser, with smaller squares. On a 3.5 × 2 inch card that matters. Fewer fields means a code that scans faster from further away.

  • Include: name, company, mobile, email, website.
  • Skip: fax, a second phone, the full postal address, long job titles.
  • Use Medium error correction unless you add a logo (the generator switches to High automatically when you do, which makes the code denser).

Printing

  • Size: at least 2 cm (0.8 in) wide; 2.5 cm (1 in) is safer for a vCard with five or six fields. Below about 2 cm many phones struggle.
  • Quiet zone: leave a clear margin of four modules (the small squares) on every side. The generator includes it; tell your designer not to crop it.
  • Colour: dark on light, with strong contrast. Avoid metallic foil or spot-UV gloss over the code; reflections stop cameras reading it.
  • Proof: scan the printed proof, not just the screen, before approving a run of 500.

FAQ

What is a vCard QR code?

A QR code containing a small contact card in the standard vCard format. Phone cameras read it and offer to save the name, phone, email and other details as a contact.

Should my business card QR code be a vCard or a link?

A vCard works offline and never depends on a website. A link keeps the code smaller and editable but only works while the page exists. For most people, a vCard with five or six fields is the better choice.

How big should the QR code be on a business card?

At least 2 cm (0.8 in) wide, and about 2.5 cm (1 in) for a vCard with several fields, with a clear four-module margin around it.

Can I update my details after printing?

Not with a vCard code: the details are inside it. You would reprint the card anyway when your details change, so this is rarely a problem.
