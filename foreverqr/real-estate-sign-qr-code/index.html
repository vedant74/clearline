QR codes for real estate signs

A buyer walking past your sign wants photos and a price, now. A QR code on the rider gets them there, if it’s big enough to scan from the sidewalk and points somewhere that will still exist after the sale.

Opens the generator set to a website link with the caption “Scan for photos and price”. Paste the listing page address.

Make a listing QR code

Choose a link that outlives the listing

Signs and riders get reused, and listings get taken down. The address inside a static code can’t change, so pick one you control:

  • Best: a page on your own or your brokerage’s site, such as yourname.com/123-maple. When the home sells, change the page to “Sold, see my other listings” instead of letting the code hit an error.
  • Good for reusable riders: a single address like yourname.com/listings that always shows your current homes. One rider, every property.
  • Risky: a portal listing URL, which may disappear when the listing is removed, or a redirect from a QR service that could be switched off when a subscription or trial ends.

Check your brokerage’s and MLS’s advertising rules, and your state’s licensing rules, for what has to appear on signs and the pages they link to.

How big to print

A common rule of thumb is that a code scans from about 10 times its own width. That makes the size question simple arithmetic:

Code widthComfortable scan distanceUse
5 cm (2 in)about 0.5 mFlyer box, feature sheet
15 cm (6 in)about 1.5 m6-inch rider, walker at the sign
30 cm (12 in)about 3 mMain yard sign, from the sidewalk

Nobody will scan a code from a moving car, so don’t try to size for the street. Design for someone who has stopped on the sidewalk, and place the code at a height where a phone can be held level with it.

Track scans without a subscription

Static codes don’t count scans, but your website can. Add campaign tags to the link before making the code, for example:

https://yourname.com/123-maple?utm_source=yard-sign&utm_medium=qr&utm_campaign=123-maple

Analytics tools such as Google Analytics report visits with these tags as their own source and campaign, so you can see how many people arrived from the sign versus the flyer box (use utm_source=flyer on that one). Your data stays in your own analytics.

  1. Publish the listing page and add the UTM tags.

Outdoor tips

  • Matte vinyl or corrugated plastic scans better than gloss in sunlight.
  • Black on white is the safest choice outdoors. Brand colours are fine if they’re dark; the generator warns about weak contrast.
  • Keep the margin. A clear border four squares wide on every side helps phones find the code against a busy sign.
  • Check after storms and sun: faded or scratched codes stop scanning before they look ruined.

FAQ

How big should a QR code be on a real estate sign?

Use roughly 10 times the code width as the scanning distance: about 15 cm (6 in) for a rider read from 1.5 m, and about 30 cm (12 in) to scan from the sidewalk at 3 m.

What should a real estate QR code link to?

A page you control, such as a listing page on your own or your brokerage’s site, so you can update it when the home sells instead of the code leading to an error.

Can I track how many people scan my yard sign?

Yes. Add UTM campaign tags to the link before making the code and check the visits in your website analytics.

Will the QR code stop working when the listing sells?

The code itself never expires. What changes is the page; point it to your own site and update that page after the sale.
