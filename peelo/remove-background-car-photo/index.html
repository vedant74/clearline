Remove the background from a car photo

Selling a car privately? A clean background makes your listing look cared-for and puts the attention on the car, not the bins and the neighbour's van. Cars bring their own problems too: windows, reflections and shadows. Here's how to handle them.

Clean background, honest photo

A background swap should tidy up the scene, not change the car. Buyers notice when listing photos hide things, and it costs trust when they arrive for a viewing. Keep the car's own pixels as they are. Peelo only changes what's behind the car. Show dents and scuffs in separate close-ups. A clean main photo plus honest detail shots sells better than a flawless-looking one.

Windows and glass

This is where every background remover struggles, and a small on-device model more than most. Through the windows you can see the original background: a hedge or a house. The model may:

  • keep the windows with the old scene visible through them, which usually looks fine and natural, or
  • cut holes where the windows are, so the new background shows through, or
  • do a bit of both, which looks odd.

The quickest fix is to choose one look and use the brush. In Refine edges, Restore over the windows to keep them solid. That's usually the most realistic option, since real glass reflects its surroundings anyway.

Shadows and "floating" cars

When a car is cut out from the tarmac, its shadow goes too, and on a plain background the car can look like it's hovering. Two things help:

  • Use a light, warm grey (like the preset) rather than stark white. It reads as a studio floor and makes the missing shadow less obvious.
  • Keep the tyres sitting on the bottom edge if you use square framing. When the car touches the bottom of your original photo, Peelo keeps it anchored there instead of floating it in the middle.

Or use Blur: it keeps the real ground and shadow but softens the driveway clutter, like a portrait-mode photo.

Number plates

Many private sellers hide the plate in online photos. Since Peelo runs on your device, the plate is never uploaded during editing. To hide it in the final image, use a photo editor or markup tool after downloading. The background tool won't cover it for you.

Shooting tips

  • Shoot on an overcast day or in open shade. Hard sun creates reflections the model may treat as background.
  • Use the classic three-quarter front angle at headlight height, with the whole car in frame and a margin around it.
  • Move the car away from other cars and objects so its outline is clear.
  • Clean it first. Mud and water spots still show after a background change.

How to do it with Peelo

  1. Open the tool with the studio grey preset and add a car photo. Nothing is uploaded.
  2. Check the windows and wheels with the slider. Use Refine edges, then Restore, to keep the windows solid, and Erase to remove ground left between the tyres.
  3. Choose the background: studio grey, white or Blur. Try Square, centred for listing sites with square thumbnails.
  4. Download JPG at full resolution for the listing.

Questions

Why are the car's windows see-through after removing the background?

Glass is transparent, so the model may treat the scene behind it as background. Use Refine edges, then Restore, to paint the windows back for a solid, realistic look.

My car looks like it's floating. How do I fix that?

The cut-out removes the ground shadow. Use a light warm grey instead of pure white, keep the tyres on the bottom edge, or use the Blur background, which keeps the real ground.

Is it okay to edit the background on a car listing?

Changing the background is common, but don't alter the car itself. Show any damage in separate photos so the listing stays accurate.
