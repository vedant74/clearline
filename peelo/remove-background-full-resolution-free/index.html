Remove backgrounds at full resolution, free

The usual catch with free background removers is the download. The preview is free, but the full-size file costs credits or a subscription. Peelo's download keeps the full resolution of your photo, free. Here's why that's possible and where the limits are.

Why other tools charge for HD

Server-based removers pay for GPU time on every image they process, and large images cost more. A low-resolution free preview with paid HD downloads is a sensible business model for them. Peelo doesn't have that cost. The AI runs on your device, in your browser, so processing a 12-megapixel photo costs us nothing. That's why the full-resolution download is free.

What "full resolution" means here

Your download has the same pixel dimensions as the photo you added. A 4032 × 3024 phone photo comes back as a 4032 × 3024 PNG or JPG. In detail:

  • The AI model works out the subject's outline on a 1024 × 1024 copy (320 × 320 in Fast mode), which keeps it fast enough to run in a browser.
  • That outline is smoothly upscaled to your photo's full size and shaped with the Edge softness curve.
  • It's then applied to your original pixels. The subject itself isn't resampled, so textures, text on labels and fine detail inside the subject stay as sharp as your original.
  • Your refine-brush strokes are recorded and replayed at full resolution on export, so manual fixes stay crisp.

The honest caveat: because the outline starts at 1024 × 1024, very fine edge detail like individual hairs is approximated rather than traced pixel by pixel. For products and most subjects you won't notice. For hair-heavy portraits you might, and the brush helps there.

The 16-megapixel limit

Browsers limit how large a canvas can be, and iPhone Safari is the strictest, failing at around 16.7 megapixels. To make sure the export never fails silently, photos above 16 MP (for example 4900 × 3265) are scaled down to 16 MP, and the tool tells you before it happens. Most phone photos (12 MP) and many camera photos fit without any scaling.

PNG or JPG?

  • PNG keeps transparency and is lossless. It's the right choice for cut-outs you'll put on other backgrounds. Files are larger, often 5 to 15 MB at 12 MP.
  • JPG flattens onto the background you chose (white by default) and is much smaller. It's the right choice for listings, email and anywhere transparency isn't needed.

Check the resolution yourself

After processing, the line under the download buttons shows the exact output size. After downloading, check it in your file's info: Get Info on a Mac, Properties → Details on Windows, or the info panel in your phone's photo app.

How to do it with Peelo

  1. Open Peelo and add your highest-resolution original, not a screenshot or a messaging-app copy, which are often compressed.
  2. Check the size shown under the download buttons. That's your output resolution.
  3. Refine edges if needed. Brush strokes are replayed at full resolution.
  4. Download PNG for transparency or JPG for a smaller file on a solid background.

Questions

Is the download really full resolution and free?

Yes. The output has the same pixel dimensions as your photo, up to 16 megapixels, with no account, credits or watermark. It's free because processing runs on your device instead of a paid server.

Why was my photo scaled down?

Photos over 16 megapixels are scaled to 16 MP because browsers, especially iPhone Safari, can't reliably create larger canvases. The tool warns you when this happens.

Does the AI upscale or sharpen my photo?

No. It only decides which pixels are background. Your subject's pixels are your original pixels, not upscaled or regenerated.

Why is my PNG file so large?

PNG is lossless and stores transparency, so a 12 MP cut-out can be 5 to 15 MB. Use JPG on a solid background for a much smaller file.
