Remove the background from a pet photo

Cut-out pet photos turn up everywhere: stickers, birthday cards, memorial prints, custom mugs, and the cat on the company Slack. Fur is the tricky part. Here's how to get a natural cut-out that doesn't look like a paper doll.

What you're making decides the settings

  • Stickers and custom prints: transparent PNG at the highest resolution you have. Print shops usually want 300 dpi at the final size, so a 10 cm sticker needs about 1200 px across.
  • Memorial or portrait prints: a soft solid colour or a gentle blur often looks warmer than pure white.
  • Social posts and profile pictures: square framing with a bright colour.
  • Pet products and listings (collars, beds, clothing): white or off-white, so the product, not the background, gets the attention.

Fur: what to expect

Background removers find soft, wispy edges hardest, and fur is all soft, wispy edges. Peelo uses a compact model that runs on your device, so be realistic. Short-haired pets on a contrasting background come out well. A long-haired white cat on a cream sofa won't be perfect. You can still get a natural result:

  • Raise Edge softness (around 50 to 70). A slightly soft outline reads as fur, while a razor-sharp one looks like it was cut with scissors.
  • Pick a background close to the old one in brightness. Fur edges blended against a similar tone hide much better than against a high-contrast colour.
  • Accept losing whiskers. Single whiskers are thinner than a pixel at the model's working size. Restore them by hand with a small brush if they matter to you.
  • Try Blur instead of a new background. It keeps the original edge colours, which hides almost every fur artefact.

Take a photo that cuts out well

  • Get down to your pet's eye level. It looks better and separates them from the floor.
  • Choose a background that contrasts with their coat: a dark dog against a light wall, a white cat against a darker blanket.
  • Use daylight and avoid flash, which gives flat fur and glowing eyes.
  • Keep their whole outline in frame. Cropped ears or paws can't be invented later.
  • Take a burst. One frame will have the ears up and the eyes open.

Leads, toys and other pets

The model looks for the main subject, so a lead, a toy in the mouth or a second pet may be kept or dropped unpredictably. Use Restore to keep things you want, like the toy, and Erase for things you don't, like a hand holding the lead.

How to do it with Peelo

  1. Open Peelo and add your pet photo. It's processed on your device.
  2. Raise Edge softness to around 50 to 70 for fur, and check the outline with the slider.
  3. Fix ears, tails and paws with Refine edges and then Restore, using a small brush for fine bits.
  4. Choose the output: transparent for stickers and gifts, or a colour or Blur for prints and posts. Square framing works well for profile pictures.
  5. Download PNG (transparent) or JPG (on a colour) at full resolution.

Questions

Why does my pet's fur look jagged or blurry?

Fur is the hardest case for background removal. Raise Edge softness for a natural edge, pick a background similar in brightness to the original, or use the Blur background, which hides edge artefacts best.

Can I make a sticker from my pet photo?

Yes. Download a transparent PNG. For printing, check the shop's size needs, typically 300 dpi at the final size, and start from your highest-resolution photo.

The whiskers disappeared. Can I get them back?

Single whiskers are often too thin for the model. Use Refine edges, then Restore, with a small brush to paint them back, or keep the Blur background, which preserves the original edges.

Does it work with two pets in one photo?

Often, but not always. The model looks for the main subject. Use Restore to bring back a second pet if it gets dropped.
